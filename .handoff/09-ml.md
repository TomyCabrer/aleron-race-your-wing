# Task 9 — "machine learning to teach the cars how to drive the aero"

`drive/ml/` — a new sub-package. **Strictly additive**: nothing in `drive/`
imports it, the 1 kHz physics path gains no import and no nondeterminism, and
the sim behaves identically with it absent or disabled. The self-check proves
the first of those by importing `drive.drive / vehicle / render / input` in a
subprocess and asserting no `drive.ml` module appears in `sys.modules`.

`python3 -m drive.ml` → **ALL PASS, 13/13**  (11 before wave 3; the two new
ones guard the multi-track fitness -- see the trainer note below).

## Shape of it

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `baseline.py` | a hand-written driver: pure pursuit on the centreline, curvature-limited target speed, a wing rule. Nothing learned. |
| `policy.py` | a 14 → 16 → 4 tanh net, 308 parameters, as a **residual** on the baseline |
| `env.py` | observation, reward, termination; one deterministic rollout |
| `train.py` | mirrored-sampling ES on centred ranks, numpy only, multiprocessing; `--track arena,open` trains one policy on several circuits |
| `evaluate.py` | baseline vs learned + a wing on/off ablation at 1 ms, the **transfer grid** (every track x every wing, `--transfer`), and the learning-curve plot |
| `__main__.py` | the self-check |
| `checkpoints/` | trained policies, committed (`runs/` is gitignored, so they cannot live there): `arena_plate`, `arena_open_plate`, `open_plate` |

## The decisions worth arguing with

**An ES, not deep RL.** `torch` *is* importable in this environment, but the
repo uses none of it and the brief is to add no dependency. An ES needs only
numpy, has no backward pass to get wrong, is trivially parallel over a process
pool, and does not care that episode reward is a non-differentiable function of
a 1 kHz ODE integration. The binding constraint here is rollout throughput,
not sample efficiency — and it turned out not to bind at all: 32 rollouts of
70 simulated seconds take ~14 s of wall clock across 12 cores.

**The policy is a RESIDUAL on a hand-written driver.** `theta = 0` is exactly
`baseline.baseline_action`, verified to 0.0e+00 over 64 random observations.
This is the single most important design choice in the task. Trained from
zeros the ES spends its whole budget discovering that grass is slow; anchored
on a competent driver it spends it on **the line and the aero**, which is what
the owner actually asked for. It also gives an honest *before* number for free:
the baseline and the learned policy are the same code path with and without the
residual.

**Training at dt = 2 ms, every quoted number at dt = 1 ms.** Measured drift
against 1 ms over 20 s of the same inputs:

```
dt = 1 ms   16.8x real time    0.000 m      the contract's DT_PHYS
dt = 2 ms   33.6x real time    0.128 m      <- training
dt = 4 ms   67.2x real time    2.618 m
dt = 8 ms  133.1x real time   25.041 m
```

2 ms buys a factor of two for 13 cm over 20 s. 4 ms does not buy anything worth
2.6 m. Nothing reported to a human is measured at the training timestep.

**Centred ranks, not raw rewards.** The reward has a −60 cliff for leaving the
track. With raw rewards one crashed candidate dominates the gradient estimate
and the population collapses onto "drive slowly"; ranks are scale-free and
outlier-proof. Observed directly in an early run.

**One pedal axis.** `pedal > 0` is throttle and `pedal < 0` is brake, so the
policy *cannot* command both — structural, not learned. A two-headed output
learned to press both at once within a few iterations.

**No reward term for the wing at all.** Not for deploying it, not for its drag.
The drag is already in the physics and charging it twice would teach the policy
to stow a device that is earning its keep. Whether to deploy has to fall out of
lap time or it means nothing.

**The policy controls the wing's deployment but not directly its side.**
`Controls.wing_on` is the driver's boolean toggle; `sgn_dev` — which flank —
is derived inside `vehicle.py` from the sign of the steering command
(CONTRACT section 4, reconciliation 7). So the agent chooses *when* to arm the
device and, through its steering, *which* side it lands on. That is the same
authority a human driver has, and giving the policy a direct side output would
mean inventing an actuator the car does not have.

## Observation (14) — nothing a human at the HUD could not see

`u`, lateral offset `n` from the centreline, heading error, centreline
curvature here and at 15 / 35 / 70 m, `beta`, `r`, `ay`, `util_f`, `util_r`,
the flank panel's deploy fraction, and an on-tarmac flag. Every one is either
on CONTRACT section 4's published read-only attribute list or a pure function
of `track.project`. Nothing is smuggled out of the integrator.

## Results

Baseline, arena, plate fitted, measured at 1 ms over 280 s:
**best lap 64.081 s**, 4 laps, `v_mean` 19.416 m/s, panel deployed 62 % of the
time with **97 % of that on the outer flank** — which is the task-4 latch fix
working, and would have read very differently before it.

**Trained**: 90 ES iterations x population 32 = **2880 rollouts in 1398 s**
(23 min, 12 cores). Checkpoint `drive/ml/checkpoints/arena_plate.json`,
learning curve `runs/ml_learning.png` (regenerate with
`python3 -m drive.ml.evaluate <ckpt> --plot runs/ml_learning.png`).

Measured at **dt = 1 ms** on the arena, plate fitted, 280 s, against current
`main` (so both rows include the task-5 gearbox fixes and the task-8 steering
fixes):

```
             best lap  laps  v_mean   v_max     dist  wing%  outer%  ended
  baseline     64.565     4  19.370  29.909   5379.1   62.0    97.1  time
  learned      58.609     4  20.999  30.082   5905.2   56.0    97.2  time
  -> +5.956 s a lap (+9.22 %)
```

**Read that as an arena/plate number, not as "the policy is 9.22 % faster".**
It was trained on that one cell and it is the best case of a band. The
corrected headline, from the transfer grid below:

> **A learned residual is 4.9 to 9.2 % a lap faster than the hand-written
> driver, on tracks it did and did not train on, with every flank device and
> with none.** 9.22 % is a specialist on its own circuit; 4.86 % is a policy
> that has never seen the arena, lapping the arena. Of the 12 (policy, cell)
> pairs where the baseline laps and a percentage means anything, the learned
> policy is faster in 11 and 1.39 % slower in one -- and in that one it still
> completes 16 laps without leaving the road.

Three checkpoints now, and they are different trades, not versions:

| checkpoint | trained on | cells of 9 it laps | its number |
|---|---|---|---|
| `arena_plate.json` | arena, plate | 6 | **58.609 s** on the arena, the fastest lap in the study |
| `arena_open_plate.json` | arena + open, plate | **8** | laps `open` (69.175 s) where `theta = 0` is off at 451.5 m |
| `open_plate.json` | open, plate | 7 | **+4.86 %** on an arena it has never seen |

Reward during training rose from the baseline's 1335 m to 1455 m of
centreline advanced in 70 s (+9.0 %), which is the same number seen from the
training side. Most of the gain arrives in the first ~5 iterations and it is
converged by ~40; a longer run is not obviously worth it at this population.

### Has it learned to use the aero?

`--ablation` puts the question directly, running each policy with the device
fitted and with it removed:

```
              wing off  wing plate     delta
  baseline       549.2      5379.1   +4829.9 m (distance: no flying lap)
  learned       59.246      58.609    +0.637 s
```

Two things fall out of that, and the second is the more interesting:

* **The learned policy is 0.637 s a lap faster with the panel than without
  it** — about 1.1 %, which is the right order for a device the study values
  at +2.35 % of *corner speed*, since a lap is not all corner.
* **The baseline cannot lap at all without the device** (549 m, then off the
  track), while the learned policy laps in 59.246 s without it. The
  hand-written driver had come to *depend* on the extra front grip its own
  wing rule was buying; the learned policy is a better driver first and
  exploits the wing second. That is the healthier result, and it is not what
  I expected before running it.
* The learned policy deploys the panel **less** than the baseline (56 % of
  steps against 62 %) and still goes faster — so part of what it learned is
  when *not* to carry the drag. 97 % of deployments are on the outer flank in
  both, which is the task-4 latch fix holding.

**The arena/plate row reproduces the committed numbers to the millisecond**

stock Corsa, which is what it was supposed to do.

### 1. Another track: it transfers to `skidpad`, and `open` cannot answer

**`skidpad` (+7.51 %, 15.775 s against the baseline's 17.055 s).** A
constant-radius R = 50 m circle the policy has never seen, 20 m wide instead of
12, at a steady ~19 m/s instead of the arena's braking and accelerating. Most
of the arena gain -- 7.5 of the 9.2 points -- survives on it. That is the
result that says the policy learned *a corner*, not *the arena's corners*.

**`open`: the test is void, and the reason is the hand-written baseline.**
Both drivers leave the track at the same place, 451.5 m (baseline) and 464.1 m
(learned) into the lap, and neither posts a lap. That is not the policy
failing to transfer; it is the *anchor* failing, measured:

```
  open geometry:   length 1642.743 m, R_min 45.0 m, longest straight 420.1 m -> R 45 m
  arena geometry:  length 1249.202 m, R_min 30.0 m, longest straight 169.5 m -> R 45 m
```

Same corner radius. Four hundred and twenty metres of run-up instead of a
hundred and seventy. `baseline_action` plans its speed off
`k_plan = max(|k0|, |k1|, 0.8|k2|)` where `k0/k1/k2` are `obs[3]/obs[4]/obs[5]`
-- **the 0 / 15 / 35 m lookaheads. It never reads `obs[6]`, the 70 m one.**
Instrumented, the baseline therefore holds full throttle to s = 385 m
(`k70` has read 0.02222 since s = 350 m and nothing in the speed plan looks at
it), goes to full brake for exactly 35 m, arrives at the corner at 22.21 m/s
against the 18.20 m/s its own 0.75 g plan asks for, runs wide, and is off at
s = 451.5 m with `n = -7.20` m. Braking 32.3 -> 18.2 m/s at the measured
0.82 g needs 44.5 m; it gives itself 35.

One caveat on how that failure is scored, because it cuts the other way.
`env.MARGIN_OFF` terminates the episode at 1.2 m past the **ribbon** edge, and
on `open` the ribbon is a 12 m road painted across a 522 x 362 m pad that is
tarmac everywhere (`make_open`'s `rrect` Area). A human running wide there
would stay on tarmac and carry on; the rollout kills the episode. Both drivers
are judged by the same rule so the comparison is fair, but "off at 451.5 m" on
`open` means *off the painted road*, not off the pad -- on the arena and the
skidpad, off the ribbon really is grass.

`policy.py`'s observation *does* carry the 70 m lookahead -- the network sees
it, the hand-written driver does not -- so this is a gap in the baseline and
the policy is free to close it. **The obvious one-line fix (put `0.6*|k2_70m|`
into `k_plan`) was deliberately NOT applied**: `baseline_action` is the origin
of the residual, so changing it changes what `theta = 0` means and silently
invalidates `arena_plate.json` and every number above it. The honest move is
to leave the anchor alone, record that `open` is not a usable transfer test for
*this* baseline, and make "can the ES learn to brake for a corner its anchor
drives straight off?" the second training run -- which is exactly what
`arena_open_plate.json` below does.

### 2. Another aero configuration: it transfers, and the asymmetry is the story

`--wing off` and `--wing fin` on the arena, against the `plate` it trained on:

| arena, dt 1 ms | baseline | learned |
|---|---|---|
| wing `plate` (trained) | 64.565 s | **58.609 s** |
| wing `fin` (unseen) | no lap, off at 562.3 m | **58.775 s** (+0.166 s vs plate) |
| wing `off` (unseen) | no lap, off at 549.2 m | **59.246 s** (+0.637 s vs plate) |

The learned policy laps in all three, within 1.1 % of itself. The baseline
laps in **none** of the two it was not swept on -- its 36-point gain sweep was
run with the plate fitted and it has come to depend on the front grip its own
wing rule buys. So the aero transfer is not "the gain survives", it is
stronger: **the policy is device-agnostic where the hand-written driver is
device-dependent**, and it still prefers the plate (0.637 s a lap over nothing,
0.166 s over the fin) when it has one.

### 3. Is a failed transfer SAFE? (the residual question)

This is why the residual design was chosen, so it deserves a straight answer.

**Yes. Measured over 27 (policy, cell) pairs, a policy on a track it never saw
degrades gracefully toward the hand-written driver and never drives worse than
it in any way that matters.** Three findings, in order of how much they are
worth:

1. **The single worst regression anywhere in the grid is -1.39 % (-0.237 s a
   lap)**: `arena_plate` on `skidpad` with the wing removed, 17.295 s against
   the baseline's 17.058 s. In that cell -- an unseen track **and** an unseen
   aero configuration at once -- it still completed **16 laps in 280 s and
   never left the track**, ending on the clock exactly as the baseline did. It
   gets slower. It does not get dangerous. Every other one of the 12
   comparable pairs is faster than the baseline.
2. **In every cell where the baseline cannot lap, the learned policy never
   does worse.** There are 5 such cells and 3 policies, so 15 pairs: in **9**
   of them the policy turns a baseline failure into a completed lap, and in the
   other 6 it covers **more ground than the baseline before the same failure**
   (e.g. `open/plate`: 464.1 m against 451.5 m; `arena/off`: 590.5 m against
   549.2 m). Not once does the residual take a car that the baseline was
   keeping on the road and put it off.
3. **The failures it does have are the baseline's failures, in the same place.**
   Every `offtrack` in the grid is one the baseline also has, at the same
   corner, within 41 m.

Mechanically that is what `RESID_GAIN = 0.55` on a **bounded** tanh buys.
Off-distribution the network's output is not large, it is saturated at +-1, so
the composed action stays within half a lock and half a pedal of pure pursuit
and the car goes on being driven by the hand-written driver with a trim on it.
The failure mode of a residual is *the baseline's lap time*, which is a lap
time. A policy that had replaced the baseline instead of trimming it would
have had nothing to fall back on off-distribution and would have had to be
trained on every track it was ever shown.

The honest limit of that claim: it is measured on 3 tracks x 3 wings x 3
policies with one car (the stock Corsa C) from one fixed start pose, and
`MARGIN_OFF` is the only safety criterion in it. "Does not crash" here means
"does not leave the 12 m ribbon", not "does not spin the car at the limit".

### 4. The second policy: `arena_open_plate.json`, one policy on two circuits

**Why this experiment and not "train on `open` too".** Once `open` turned out
to be a track the *anchor* drives off, "does the method work on a second
circuit" and "does one policy generalise across two" stopped being two
experiments. A joint run has to keep the arena *and* learn to brake for a
corner `theta = 0` takes flat out, so it answers the harder question and the
easier one at the same time — and unlike a second single-track checkpoint it
produces a policy whose claim is general rather than another local one. (An
`open`-only control, `open_plate.json`, is below; it is the cheaper question.)

    python3 -m drive.ml.train --track arena,open --iters 90 --pop 32 \
        --sigma 0.08 --lr 0.05 --duration 70 \
        --out drive/ml/checkpoints/arena_open_plate.json

Same hyperparameters as the arena run so the two are comparable. 90 iterations
x population 32 x **2 rollouts per candidate** = 5760 rollouts in **3493 s**
(58 min, 12 cores). Fitness = mean over the two tracks of
reward / the baseline's reward on that track; the normalisers it measured are
**arena 1335.388 m, open 391.556 m** — and note that the second of those is a
*failing* baseline (451.5 m of progress minus the 60 m off-track cliff), which
deliberately puts ~3.4x more gradient pressure per metre on the track the
anchor cannot drive. That is a choice, it is in the checkpoint's `meta.norm`,
and it is why the arena gave a little ground.

Curve (`runs/ml_learning_arena_open.png`; `runs/` is gitignored so the numbers
are here too):

```
  it  fitness  mean m  ended (arena/open)
   0   1.0010   895.1  time/offtrack     <- theta = 0 is the baseline: 1.0 by construction
   4   1.0391   937.8  time/offtrack
   5   2.6058  1522.5  time/time         <- it stops falling off open HERE, iteration 5
  32   2.6222  1534.3  time/time         <- best mean policy of the run
  89   2.6149  1530.7  time/time
```

**The mean policy stopped leaving `open` at iteration 5 and the remaining 84
iterations bought 0.6 % of fitness.** Same shape as the arena run, where most
of the gain also arrived in the first ~5 iterations: at this population the
run is converged long before 90 and 30 iterations would have done.

Measured at dt = 1 ms, 280 s, the full grid:

```

  arena     fin        --       60.381     --       --      562.3   5710.4      0      4  offtrack/time
  open      plate      --       69.175     --       --      451.5   6607.7      0      4  offtrack/time
  open      off        --       69.257     --       --      450.8   6600.3      0      4  offtrack/time
  open      fin        --       69.189     --       --      451.2   6606.3      0      4  offtrack/time
```

**It laps `open`: 69.175 s, 4 laps, 6607.7 m in 280 s, where the hand-written
driver it is a residual on is off the road at 451.5 m.** That is the strongest
single number in this note, because it is not a lap time against a baseline —
it is a corner the anchor cannot take, taken. The residual learned to brake
35 m earlier than pure pursuit tells it to, using the 70 m curvature the
observation carries and `baseline_action` ignores.

What it cost, stated plainly:

| cell | arena-only policy | arena+open policy | |
|---|---|---|---|
| arena / plate | **58.609 s** (+9.22 %) | 60.153 s (+6.83 %) | **-1.544 s a lap** |
| arena / fin | 58.775 s | 60.381 s | -1.606 s |
| arena / off | 59.246 s | **no lap**, off at 590.5 m | **lost the cell** |
| open / plate | no lap, 464.1 m | **69.175 s, 4 laps** | **won the cell** |
| open / off, fin | no lap | 69.257 / 69.189 s | **won both** |
| skidpad / plate | 15.775 s (+7.51 %) | 15.857 s (+7.02 %) | -0.082 s |
| skidpad / off | 17.295 s (**-1.39 %**) | **15.919 s (+6.68 %)** | **+1.376 s** |
| skidpad / fin | 15.792 s (+7.41 %) | 15.869 s (+6.96 %) | -0.077 s |

Cells in which a flying lap is posted at all: **baseline 4 of 9, arena-only
policy 6 of 9, arena+open policy 8 of 9.** So the two-circuit policy is the
more general driver and the arena-only one is the faster specialist, which is
exactly the trade you would predict and is worth having measured rather than
assumed. The one cell the joint policy *lost* is `arena` with no wing at all:
it is more device-dependent on the arena than the specialist is, and the
reason is visible in the fitness — every one of its 5760 training rollouts had
the plate fitted, and unlike the arena-only run it had no spare capacity to
spend on the arena's tightest corners (R = 30 m, tighter than anything on
`open`) once the open corner was paid for.

Wing use barely moved over the run: deployed 33.5 % of steps at iteration 0
and 36.6 % at 89, 92.6 % -> 92.7 % of those on the outer flank. The latch fix
holds on `open` too, which is the first time it has been checked there.

The cheap half of the question, run as a control because the joint run cannot
separate "the method works on a second circuit" from "the method works when
the arena is also in the objective":

**Verdict: the gain TRANSFERS.** Not "degrades", not "vanishes". Of the 12

specialist keeps 9.22 % on its own circuit and hands back 2 to 4 points of it
elsewhere — not its existence. **The +9.22 % headline should be read as the
best case of a 4.9 to 9.2 % band, and the band is the claim.**

### What is STILL unverified after wave 3

* **One car.** Everything above is the stock **Corsa C**. `rollout(car=...)`
  takes a car, and nothing has been trained or even evaluated on the other two
  cars in the library. A policy is 308 numbers tuned against one car's grip,
  power and brakes; there is no reason to expect it to transfer across cars and
  no measurement either way.
* **One start pose, one rollout, no noise.** Fitness is a single deterministic
  rollout from `track.start_pose(tr)` at V0 = 12 m/s in gear 2. The policy can
  therefore be fitted to that exact entry into the first corner. The transfer
  grid partly covers for this -- a memorised line for the arena cannot also be
  a memorised line for the skidpad -- but the right fix is to randomise the
  start pose and average a few rollouts, and that has not been done.
* **`skidpad` is a weak second track.** It is a constant-radius circle: it
  tests "hold the limit" and it does not test braking, turn-in or corner
  sequencing at all. `open` tests those and is the track the anchor cannot
  drive, so the only *strong* unseen-track number in the note is the reverse
  direction, `open_plate` on the arena (+4.86 %).
* **No wet surface anywhere in the grid.** The arena's patches are installed
  by default (`make_arena(surfaces=True)`) and `open`'s wet square is off the
  ribbon, so the policy has met `mu_scale < 1` on the arena only, and
  `--wet` was never varied. A policy that has learned a dry-grip speed plan is
  exactly the kind that would be dangerous in the wet.
* **The two failures of the whole study are not understood, only located.**
  `arena/off` for `arena_open_plate` (off at 590.5 m) and `arena/off`,
  `arena/fin` for `open_plate` (553.6 / 565.0 m). All three are the arena's
  R = 30 m corners without the plate's front grip; none has been instrumented
  the way the `open` failure was.
* **`policy.LOCK_RAD` is hard-wired to the Corsa's 32.625 deg of road-wheel
  lock** (`input.DELTA_LOCK_DEG`), and wave 3 made the interactive lock
  per-car (`ScriptedInput(lock_deg=...)`, `input.py:569`). Re-checked against
  the owner's latest `main`: `DELTA_LOCK_DEG` is still 32.625 and the Corsa
  row re-measures at 64.565 / 58.609 / +9.22 %, so nothing is wrong today.
  But the moment `drive.ml` is pointed at a second car, `policy.py` has to
  take the lock from the car the way `input.py` now does, or the policy's
  `steer` output silently means a different number of degrees.
* **Nothing is measured against a human or against `qss.py`.** The baseline is
  the only reference. 60.153 s on the arena may still be a long way off what
  the car can do -- there is no lap-time optimal to compare with.
* **`baseline_action`'s speed plan reads the 0 / 15 / 35 m curvature lookaheads
  and never `obs[6]`, the 70 m one.** That is why it cannot drive `open`: it
  brakes 35 m before an R = 45 m corner it enters at 32.3 m/s and needs 44.5 m.
  NOT fixed on purpose -- it is the origin of the residual, so touching it
  changes what `theta = 0` means and invalidates every checkpoint. If someone
  does fix it, every number in this note has to be re-measured and the
  checkpoints retrained, not reused.
* The reward is distance-in-fixed-time, which is a proxy for lap time and is
  exactly equivalent only for a car that completes whole laps.
* The baseline's gains came from a 36-point grid sweep, not an optimisation;
  a better baseline would leave the ES less to find.
* Deterministic single-rollout fitness means the policy can overfit the exact
  racing line from the exact start pose. That is fine for a lap-time objective
  and wrong for a robust driver; a proper version would randomise the start.
* No car other than the Corsa C was used (the car library landed in parallel);
  `rollout(car=...)` takes one, so this is a wiring exercise, not a redesign.

## Wave 3 follow-ups applied by the integrator

**`policy.LOCK_RAD` no longer hard-wires the Corsa's lock.** Flagged by the
item-3 work as "fine today; silently wrong the moment another car is used",
and it was: `vehicle.py` takes the road-wheel lock from the car
(`car_lock_rad`) and `input.py` from `lock_deg`, so a policy driving an MX-5
would have commanded 32.625° of a 31.2° rack. `Policy.action` and
`Policy.controls` now take a `lock_rad` argument defaulting to `LOCK_RAD`, and
`env.rollout` passes `veh.lock_rad`. For the Corsa `car_lock_rad(car) ==
LOCK_RAD` **exactly**, so every committed checkpoint's measured numbers are
unmoved — re-verified after the change: `arena_plate` on arena/plate is
64.565 → 58.609 s, **+9.22 %**, identical. `python3 -m drive.ml` 13/13.

**The baseline's 70 m blind spot is deliberately NOT fixed.** `baseline_action`
plans its speed from `obs[3]/obs[4]/obs[5]` only and never `obs[6]`, which is
why it drives off `open` at 451.5 m. The reasoning for leaving it stands and I
endorse it: the baseline **is** the origin of the residual, so changing it
redefines `theta = 0` and invalidates all three committed checkpoints and every
number in this note. It is queued, not forgotten, and the patch is one line:

```python
k_plan = max(abs(k0), abs(k1), 0.8 * abs(k2), 0.6 * abs(obs[6] / 50.0))
```

Applying it means retraining `arena_plate`, `arena_open_plate` and
`open_plate` (~1.7 h of compute all told) and re-measuring the transfer grid.
Worth doing next time this package is touched; **not** worth doing to make a
number look better, since the headline claim is measured *against* this
baseline and is therefore conservative rather than flattering — a better
anchor would shrink the reported gain, not grow it.

**On running the suite during training.** The item-3 work saw
`validate --modules` report 99/100 with `drive.render` exiting 1 under a

## Item 1a: the anchor now reads the 70 m lookahead — and 0.6 was wrong

The patch queued at the bottom of the wave-3 note was


and the coefficient in it **does not work**. It is now
`baseline.K70_PLAN`, a named constant rather than a literal, precisely
because it needed sweeping. Baseline (`theta = 0`) at dt = 1 ms over 280 s
with the plate fitted:

```
    c      arena                 open

```

Two cliffs, ten hundredths apart from the shipped value on each side: below
0.35 the driver still cannot brake for `open`, and **at 0.6 and above it can
no longer drive the arena it has driven since wave 1.** Shipping 0.45, the
middle of the usable band. It costs 0.31 s a lap on the arena against the

`skidpad` is flat in `c` to the millisecond (17.055 s at every value swept).
A constant-radius circle has the same curvature at 0, 15, 35 and 70 m, so the
`max` cannot change and the new term is provably inert there. That is a useful
null result: it says the coefficient only ever acts where curvature *changes*.

### Why 0.6 puts the BASELINE off the arena — instrumented, not assumed

The surprising half. A *slower* driver running wide wants explaining, so it
was traced (both runs, dt = 1 ms, logged every 50 steps):

```
    t      s(0.60)   n(0.40)   n(0.60)   u(0.40)   u(0.60)    mu
  53.50     994.4     -0.96     -2.09     24.68     22.40    0.80
  54.00    1004.5     -1.05     -4.33     24.27     20.21    0.80
  54.50    1014.0     -0.60     -6.06     23.09     18.34    0.55
  55.05    1023.5     +0.04     -7.19     19.35     16.49    0.55   <- off
```

The arena's **`mu = 0.55` surface patch sits at s ≈ 1010..1030 m, about 70 m
after the preceding corner.** At c ≥ 0.6 the 70 m station therefore puts the
driver *on the brakes* (0.28, then 0.66) exactly as it turns in on grip
falling 0.80 → 0.55. It spends its friction circle longitudinally, runs wide,
and the pure-pursuit feedback saturates at full lock (0.569 rad) with nothing
left to recover on. At c = 0.45 the car is **2.3 m/s faster** through the same
patch and holds n = −1.0 m where c = 0.60 is at −6.1 m and gone.

So this is not "the plan got too conservative". It is combined braking on a
low-mu patch, and it is the first time anything in this study has been caught
by the arena's surfaces rather than by its geometry. Worth the fifteen minutes
it took to instrument.

### What the wiring needed (no measurement in it)

`baseline_action` takes the car's **wheelbase**: its feedforward is the bicycle
angle `L * kappa`, so a fixed 2.491 m gave the 540i 14 % too much steering
feedforward and the MX-5 10 % too little — a systematic bias that would have
read as a policy result. `lap_time` takes `car=` (`rollout` always did, but the
only function that reports a *lap time* could not, so every number in this note
above was a Corsa number by construction rather than by choice). `train` takes
`--car`. Both per-car arguments go to the **anchor only** — the network reads
neither, so its 308 numbers are a trim in each car's own actuator units and a
checkpoint means the same thing on every car. For the Corsa both are exactly
the old constants, asserted to 0.0e+00, so nothing above moved for that reason.
