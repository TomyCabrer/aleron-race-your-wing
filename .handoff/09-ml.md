# Task 9 — "machine learning to teach the cars how to drive the aero"

`drive/ml/` — a new sub-package. **Strictly additive**: nothing in `drive/`
imports it, the 1 kHz physics path gains no import and no nondeterminism, and
the sim behaves identically with it absent or disabled. The self-check proves
the first of those by importing `drive.drive / vehicle / render / input` in a
subprocess and asserting no `drive.ml` module appears in `sys.modules`.

`python3 -m drive.ml` → **ALL PASS, 13/13**  (11 before wave 3; the two new
ones guard the multi-track fitness -- see the trainer note below).

## Shape of it

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `baseline.py` | a hand-written driver: pure pursuit on the centreline, curvature-limited target speed, a wing rule. Nothing learned. |
| `policy.py` | a 14 → 16 → 4 tanh net, 308 parameters, as a **residual** on the baseline |
| `env.py` | observation, reward, termination; one deterministic rollout |
| `train.py` | mirrored-sampling ES on centred ranks, numpy only, multiprocessing |
| `evaluate.py` | baseline vs learned + a wing on/off ablation at 1 ms, and the learning-curve plot |
| `__main__.py` | the self-check |
| `checkpoints/` | trained policies, committed (`runs/` is gitignored, so they cannot live there) |

## The decisions worth arguing with

**An ES, not deep RL.** `torch` *is* importable in this environment, but the
repo uses none of it and the brief is to add no dependency. An ES needs only
numpy, has no backward pass to get wrong, is trivially parallel over a process
pool, and does not care that episode reward is a non-differentiable function of
a 1 kHz ODE integration. The binding constraint here is rollout throughput,
not sample efficiency — and it turned out not to bind at all: 32 rollouts of
70 simulated seconds take ~14 s of wall clock across 12 cores.

**The policy is a RESIDUAL on a hand-written driver.** `theta = 0` is exactly
`baseline.baseline_action`, verified to 0.0e+00 over 64 random observations.
This is the single most important design choice in the task. Trained from
zeros the ES spends its whole budget discovering that grass is slow; anchored
on a competent driver it spends it on **the line and the aero**, which is what
the owner actually asked for. It also gives an honest *before* number for free:
the baseline and the learned policy are the same code path with and without the
residual.

**Training at dt = 2 ms, every quoted number at dt = 1 ms.** Measured drift
against 1 ms over 20 s of the same inputs:

```
dt = 1 ms   16.8x real time    0.000 m      the contract's DT_PHYS
dt = 2 ms   33.6x real time    0.128 m      <- training
dt = 4 ms   67.2x real time    2.618 m
dt = 8 ms  133.1x real time   25.041 m
```

2 ms buys a factor of two for 13 cm over 20 s. 4 ms does not buy anything worth
2.6 m. Nothing reported to a human is measured at the training timestep.

**Centred ranks, not raw rewards.** The reward has a −60 cliff for leaving the
track. With raw rewards one crashed candidate dominates the gradient estimate
and the population collapses onto "drive slowly"; ranks are scale-free and
outlier-proof. Observed directly in an early run.

**One pedal axis.** `pedal > 0` is throttle and `pedal < 0` is brake, so the
policy *cannot* command both — structural, not learned. A two-headed output
learned to press both at once within a few iterations.

**No reward term for the wing at all.** Not for deploying it, not for its drag.
The drag is already in the physics and charging it twice would teach the policy
to stow a device that is earning its keep. Whether to deploy has to fall out of
lap time or it means nothing.

**The policy controls the wing's deployment but not directly its side.**
`Controls.wing_on` is the driver's boolean toggle; `sgn_dev` — which flank —
is derived inside `vehicle.py` from the sign of the steering command
(CONTRACT section 4, reconciliation 7). So the agent chooses *when* to arm the
device and, through its steering, *which* side it lands on. That is the same
authority a human driver has, and giving the policy a direct side output would
mean inventing an actuator the car does not have.

## Observation (14) — nothing a human at the HUD could not see

`u`, lateral offset `n` from the centreline, heading error, centreline
curvature here and at 15 / 35 / 70 m, `beta`, `r`, `ay`, `util_f`, `util_r`,
the flank panel's deploy fraction, and an on-tarmac flag. Every one is either
on CONTRACT section 4's published read-only attribute list or a pure function
of `track.project`. Nothing is smuggled out of the integrator.

## Results

Baseline, arena, plate fitted, measured at 1 ms over 280 s:
**best lap 64.081 s**, 4 laps, `v_mean` 19.416 m/s, panel deployed 62 % of the
time with **97 % of that on the outer flank** — which is the task-4 latch fix
working, and would have read very differently before it.

**Trained**: 90 ES iterations x population 32 = **2880 rollouts in 1398 s**
(23 min, 12 cores). Checkpoint `drive/ml/checkpoints/arena_plate.json`,
learning curve `runs/ml_learning.png` (regenerate with
`python3 -m drive.ml.evaluate <ckpt> --plot runs/ml_learning.png`).

Measured at **dt = 1 ms** on the arena, plate fitted, 280 s, against current
`main` (so both rows include the task-5 gearbox fixes and the task-8 steering
fixes):

```
             best lap  laps  v_mean   v_max     dist  wing%  outer%  ended
  baseline     64.565     4  19.370  29.909   5379.1   62.0    97.1  time
  learned      58.609     4  20.999  30.082   5905.2   56.0    97.2  time
  -> +5.956 s a lap (+9.22 %)
```

**Read that as an arena/plate number, not as "the policy is 9.22 % faster".**
It was trained on that one cell and it is the best case. The transfer grid
below is the honest headline: **+7.51 % on a second, unseen track
(`skidpad`), +9.22 % on the one it trained on, worst case -1.39 %, and a third
track (`open`) on which neither driver can lap because the hand-written
baseline cannot.**

Reward during training rose from the baseline's 1335 m to 1455 m of
centreline advanced in 70 s (+9.0 %), which is the same number seen from the
training side. Most of the gain arrives in the first ~5 iterations and it is
converged by ~40; a longer run is not obviously worth it at this population.

### Has it learned to use the aero?

`--ablation` puts the question directly, running each policy with the device
fitted and with it removed:

```
              wing off  wing plate     delta
  baseline       549.2      5379.1   +4829.9 m (distance: no flying lap)
  learned       59.246      58.609    +0.637 s
```

Two things fall out of that, and the second is the more interesting:

* **The learned policy is 0.637 s a lap faster with the panel than without
  it** — about 1.1 %, which is the right order for a device the study values
  at +2.35 % of *corner speed*, since a lap is not all corner.
* **The baseline cannot lap at all without the device** (549 m, then off the
  track), while the learned policy laps in 59.246 s without it. The
  hand-written driver had come to *depend* on the extra front grip its own
  wing rule was buying; the learned policy is a better driver first and
  exploits the wing second. That is the healthier result, and it is not what
  I expected before running it.
* The learned policy deploys the panel **less** than the baseline (56 % of
  steps against 62 %) and still goes faster — so part of what it learned is
  when *not* to carry the drag. 97 % of deployments are on the outer flank in
  both, which is the task-4 latch fix holding.

## Does the gain transfer, or did it memorise the arena? (wave 3, item 3)

The +9.22 % above is **one track and one aero configuration**, so on its own it
is not evidence of driving. `evaluate.transfer` puts the checkpoint --
**unchanged, the same 308 numbers, no re-tuning and no warm start** -- on every
(track, wing) cell, baseline and learned in each cell:

    python3 -m drive.ml.evaluate drive/ml/checkpoints/arena_plate.json --only-transfer

```
  transfer grid, dt 1 ms, 280 s, trained on arena / plate
  track     wing    base lap learn lap     delta       %   base m  learn m b.laps l.laps  base/learn ended
  arena     plate     64.565    58.609    +5.956   +9.22   5379.1   5905.2      4      4  time/time  <- trained
  arena     off        --       59.246     --       --      549.2   5821.9      0      4  offtrack/time
  arena     fin        --       58.775     --       --      562.3   5882.8      0      4  offtrack/time
  open      plate      --        --        --       --      451.5    464.1      0      0  offtrack/offtrack
  open      off        --        --        --       --      450.8    462.8      0      0  offtrack/offtrack
  open      fin        --        --        --       --      451.2    463.5      0      0  offtrack/offtrack
  skidpad   plate     17.055    15.775    +1.280   +7.51   5145.6   5556.2     16     17  time/time
  skidpad   off       17.058    17.295    -0.237   -1.39   5144.8   5031.5     16     16  time/time
  skidpad   fin       17.056    15.792    +1.264   +7.41   5145.3   5541.4     16     17  time/time
```

`dragstrip` is deliberately not in that table. It is a 1500 m straight and it
is not closed, so `lap_time` cannot report a lap on it and a policy's
cornering -- the entire question -- leaves no trace on it. Running it would
have padded the table, not tested anything.

**The arena/plate row reproduces the committed numbers to the millisecond**
(64.565 / 58.609), so nothing in the wave-3 powertrain and brake work moved the
stock Corsa, which is what it was supposed to do.

### 1. Another track: it transfers to `skidpad`, and `open` cannot answer

**`skidpad` (+7.51 %, 15.775 s against the baseline's 17.055 s).** A
constant-radius R = 50 m circle the policy has never seen, 20 m wide instead of
12, at a steady ~19 m/s instead of the arena's braking and accelerating. Most
of the arena gain -- 7.5 of the 9.2 points -- survives on it. That is the
result that says the policy learned *a corner*, not *the arena's corners*.

**`open`: the test is void, and the reason is the hand-written baseline.**
Both drivers leave the track at the same place, 451.5 m (baseline) and 464.1 m
(learned) into the lap, and neither posts a lap. That is not the policy
failing to transfer; it is the *anchor* failing, measured:

```
  open geometry:   length 1642.743 m, R_min 45.0 m, longest straight 420.1 m -> R 45 m
  arena geometry:  length 1249.202 m, R_min 30.0 m, longest straight 169.5 m -> R 45 m
```

Same corner radius. Four hundred and twenty metres of run-up instead of a
hundred and seventy. `baseline_action` plans its speed off
`k_plan = max(|k0|, |k1|, 0.8|k2|)` where `k0/k1/k2` are `obs[3]/obs[4]/obs[5]`
-- **the 0 / 15 / 35 m lookaheads. It never reads `obs[6]`, the 70 m one.**
Instrumented, the baseline therefore holds full throttle to s = 385 m
(`k70` has read 0.02222 since s = 350 m and nothing in the speed plan looks at
it), goes to full brake for exactly 35 m, arrives at the corner at 22.21 m/s
against the 18.20 m/s its own 0.75 g plan asks for, runs wide, and is off at
s = 451.5 m with `n = -7.20` m. Braking 32.3 -> 18.2 m/s at the measured
0.82 g needs 44.5 m; it gives itself 35.

One caveat on how that failure is scored, because it cuts the other way.
`env.MARGIN_OFF` terminates the episode at 1.2 m past the **ribbon** edge, and
on `open` the ribbon is a 12 m road painted across a 522 x 362 m pad that is
tarmac everywhere (`make_open`'s `rrect` Area). A human running wide there
would stay on tarmac and carry on; the rollout kills the episode. Both drivers
are judged by the same rule so the comparison is fair, but "off at 451.5 m" on
`open` means *off the painted road*, not off the pad -- on the arena and the
skidpad, off the ribbon really is grass.

`policy.py`'s observation *does* carry the 70 m lookahead -- the network sees
it, the hand-written driver does not -- so this is a gap in the baseline and
the policy is free to close it. **The obvious one-line fix (put `0.6*|k2_70m|`
into `k_plan`) was deliberately NOT applied**: `baseline_action` is the origin
of the residual, so changing it changes what `theta = 0` means and silently
invalidates `arena_plate.json` and every number above it. The honest move is
to leave the anchor alone, record that `open` is not a usable transfer test for
*this* baseline, and make "can the ES learn to brake for a corner its anchor
drives straight off?" the second training run -- which is exactly what
`arena_open_plate.json` below does.

### 2. Another aero configuration: it transfers, and the asymmetry is the story

`--wing off` and `--wing fin` on the arena, against the `plate` it trained on:

| arena, dt 1 ms | baseline | learned |
|---|---|---|
| wing `plate` (trained) | 64.565 s | **58.609 s** |
| wing `fin` (unseen) | no lap, off at 562.3 m | **58.775 s** (+0.166 s vs plate) |
| wing `off` (unseen) | no lap, off at 549.2 m | **59.246 s** (+0.637 s vs plate) |

The learned policy laps in all three, within 1.1 % of itself. The baseline
laps in **none** of the two it was not swept on -- its 36-point gain sweep was
run with the plate fitted and it has come to depend on the front grip its own
wing rule buys. So the aero transfer is not "the gain survives", it is
stronger: **the policy is device-agnostic where the hand-written driver is
device-dependent**, and it still prefers the plate (0.637 s a lap over nothing,
0.166 s over the fin) when it has one.

### 3. Is a failed transfer SAFE? (the residual question)

This is why the residual design was chosen, so it deserves a straight answer.

**Yes -- measured, degradation is graceful, and the worst case over every cell
where a lap was posted at all is -1.39 % (-0.237 s a lap).**

The only cell in the whole grid where the learned policy is slower than the
baseline is `skidpad` with the wing removed: 17.295 s against 17.055 s. In that
cell -- an unseen track **and** an unseen aero configuration at once -- it still
completed **16 laps in 280 s and never left the track**, ending on the clock
exactly as the baseline did. It gets slower; it does not get dangerous.

On `open`, where the anchor itself drives off, the residual carries the car
**12.6 m further** (464.1 m against 451.5 m) before the same failure. So it did
not rescue a broken baseline, and it did not make it worse either.

Mechanically that is what `RESID_GAIN = 0.55` on a tanh buys: off-distribution
the network's output is *bounded*, not large, so the composed action stays
within half a lock of the hand-written driver's and the car keeps being driven
by pure pursuit with a trim on it. A policy that had replaced the baseline
rather than trimmed it would have had nothing to fall back to on the skidpad,
and the honest comparison is that it would then have had to be trained there.

## How to watch it drive

    python3 -m drive.ml.evaluate drive/ml/checkpoints/arena_plate.json --ablation
    python3 -m drive.drive --ml-drive drive/ml/checkpoints/arena_plate.json

The second wraps the loaded policy in `input.ScriptedInput` — the closure the
acceptance scripts already use (CONTRACT section 6) — so the agent sits exactly
where a scripted driver sits and `Controls` reaches `Vehicle.step` by the path
it always did. No new physics path, no new hook inside the integrator.

## One operational trap found the hard way

**Do not run `python3 -m drive.validate --modules` while an ES is training.**
With 12 pool workers saturating 12 cores it reported `99/100, 1 HARD` --
`python3 -m drive.render -> exit 1, 25/27` -- and re-run on an idle machine the
same check is `PASS: 27/27, exit 0`. `render`'s V22 frame-budget normaliser
says of itself that it is "load-tolerant ... under x3 load"; a 12-worker
training run is not x3. The suite was **82/82, 0 HARD, 0 soft** even under that
load, so only the `--modules` render timing check is exposed. Nothing in
`drive/ml` was involved; the fix is to run the suite when the box is idle.

## Not verified / honest limits

* ~~Trained on one track with one aero configuration~~ -- **settled in wave 3,
  see the transfer grid.** `arena_plate.json` transfers to `skidpad`
  (+7.51 %) and to both other wings (device-agnostic where the baseline is
  device-dependent), degrades by at most -1.39 % anywhere it can lap, and
  cannot be scored on `open` because the *baseline* drives off there.
  `arena_open_plate.json` is the two-circuit policy.
* **`baseline_action`'s speed plan reads the 0 / 15 / 35 m curvature lookaheads
  and never `obs[6]`, the 70 m one.** That is why it cannot drive `open`: it
  brakes 35 m before an R = 45 m corner it enters at 32.3 m/s and needs 44.5 m.
  NOT fixed on purpose -- it is the origin of the residual, so touching it
  changes what `theta = 0` means and invalidates every checkpoint. If someone
  does fix it, every number in this note has to be re-measured and the
  checkpoints retrained, not reused.
* The reward is distance-in-fixed-time, which is a proxy for lap time and is
  exactly equivalent only for a car that completes whole laps.
* The baseline's gains came from a 36-point grid sweep, not an optimisation;
  a better baseline would leave the ES less to find.
* Deterministic single-rollout fitness means the policy can overfit the exact
  racing line from the exact start pose. That is fine for a lap-time objective
  and wrong for a robust driver; a proper version would randomise the start.
* No car other than the Corsa C was used (the car library landed in parallel);
  `rollout(car=...)` takes one, so this is a wiring exercise, not a redesign.
