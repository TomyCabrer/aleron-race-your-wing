# Task 1 — "View from behind (3D)"

`C` / Settings → Camera now has a genuinely three-dimensional **Chase** mode.
It used to be the top-down view with a heading lag; it is now a perspective
camera behind and above the car.

## What it is

`Chase3D` (drive/render.py) is a pinhole camera: the eye sits `CHASE_DIST`
(6.4 m + 0.035 s/m · V, so the view opens up with speed) behind the CG and
`CHASE_HEIGHT` 2.15 m up, looking at a point 11 m ahead at bonnet height, with
a 46° vertical field of view. A world point goes to camera space through the
yaw-only basis, then `x_s = W/2 + f·(y_c/x_c)`, `y_s = H/2 − f·(z_c/x_c)` with
`f = (H/2)/tan(FOV/2)`; anything closer than `CHASE_Z_NEAR = 0.35 m` is clipped
rather than projected, which is the bug that folds a ground polygon inside out
across the screen when it straddles the eye.

`Renderer.world_to_screen` dispatches to it in chase mode, so **every existing
world layer draws itself in 3-D unchanged** — ribbon, edges, kerbs, centre
dashes, surface patches, skid marks, the open map's `Area` polygons. That was
the design choice worth making: no second drawing path to keep in step. The
layers that are genuinely different get their own `…3` functions: `_draw_sky3`
(sky / ground / horizon instead of `sc.fill`), `_draw_features3`,
`_draw_car3d` and `_draw_arrows3`.

The car, its wheels and all three wings are **one painter's-sorted, backface-
culled soup** (`wing_mesh3` + `_car_mesh3`), not a fixed layer order, so a
deployed flank panel is correctly occluded by the flank it is behind. The
wings read their state off `HudData` exactly as the 2-D `_draw_wing` does
(`dev_left/dev_right`, `x_w_left/x_w_right`, `dev_chord/dev_span/dev_plate`,
`wing_side`, `wing_deploy`, and `top_on/top_deploy/top_x/top_span/top_chord/
top_plate`), so they move with their **actual** deployment state: stowed flat
on the flank, the outer one slid out and lit while deployed, the top wing lying
on the deck and rising to its slot.

## Where the projection came from

Lifted from `garage.py`'s `GarageView` (the loft, the painter's sort, the
backface cull, the palette), **not imported**: `render.py` may not import
`garage`, because `garage` imports `vehicle` and `input` and the dependency
would invert the contract's module map. Duplicating ~200 lines of mesh code
was judged the cheaper of the two. Recorded as DEVIATION 8 in `render.py`'s
own deviations list, which the module self-check prints.

`render.py` stays strictly read-only w.r.t. physics: no physics import it did
not already have, no mutation of vehicle state, no new dependency.

## Evidence

`python3 -m drive.render` → **24/24 checks** (was 19/19), including five new
chase checks:

```
[ok] chase: perspective, not a plan view   car CG at y 612 px, 40 m ahead at y 373 px, horizon above both
[ok] chase: the road narrows with distance 12 m ribbon is 646 px at 10 m ahead, 105 px at 100 m
[ok] chase: frame budget                   4.23 ms mean over 60 frames, p99 7.89 ms (flat car_up is 2.1-2.4 ms)
[ok] chase: the OUTER flank panel is the lit one  wing_side -1 (right turn) -> lit panel on the LEFT flank
[ok] chase screenshots                     runs/render_chase3d.png, runs/render_chase3d_deployed.png
```

The two discriminators are deliberate: a *plan* projection would keep the
ribbon a constant width and would put a point 40 m ahead at the same screen
height as the car, so both checks fail if anyone ever quietly reverts the mode
to top-down. `_lit_flank_side` reads the side off the produced **mesh**, not
off the flag that built it, so it is not a tautology.

Frames (regenerated by the self-check):
* `runs/render_chase3d.png` — open-map straight, wings stowed. Vanishing
  point, horizon, both road edges converging, centre dashes, the car from
  behind with three stowed wings and the tyre-force arrows.
* `runs/render_chase3d_deployed.png` — mid-corner, armed: top wing raised and
  lit, outer (left) flank panel slid out and lit, the inner one stowed grey,
  kerb and edge lines in perspective.

Cost: **4.23 ms mean, 7.89 ms p99** against the 16.7 ms frame budget, versus
2.1–2.4 ms for the flat modes. The extra is the ~300-polygon car soup and the
per-point perspective divide. Comfortably inside 60 fps; the budget check
asserts ≤ 12 ms so a regression is caught.

## Assumptions

* The owner's "view from behind" is a chase camera, not a cockpit/bonnet view.
  If he meant a driver's-eye view, the same `Chase3D` does it by moving the eye
  to the cabin and dropping the car mesh — a handful of lines, not a rewrite.
* `wing_side` is the **turn sign** (`sgn_dev`), not the side of the car — that
  is how the existing 2-D HUD and `_draw_wing` read it, and the 3-D path
  follows them so the two views can never disagree. CONTRACT section 4's
  parenthetical "(wing_side: -1 right, 0 none, +1 left)" reads as a side index
  and is misleading; flagged to the wing audit for a wording ruling.

## Not verified

* Never run on a real window, only headless with `SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy`, so
  the frame cost is measured without a real blit/vsync.
* Camera feel (the heading lag `tau_heading × 2.5` in chase, the zoom stops)
  is tuned by eye from the two frames above, not by driving it.
* The flat `car_up` / `world_up` modes are untouched and their checks still
  pass bit-identically, but I did not diff their rendered output pixel by pixel.
